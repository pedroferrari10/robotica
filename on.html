THE CLASSICAL ROBOTICS

the origin of the robotics:

The robotics begam on the ancient greece, where the first ones mechanicals devices created by inventors such as Archytas of Tarentum and Hero of Alexandria.
Renaissance era: Designs for machines and mechanical lions sketched by Leonardo da Vinci in the 15th century.
18th Century: European clockmakers built complex automatons capable of writing and moving. And all of this was achieved using only gears, cords, and simple mechanisms—especially considering the differences between the 21st and 18th centuries.

Origin of the world and of the fiction:

1920–1921: The Czech writer Karel Čapek first used the word "robot" in the play *R.U.R.* (Rossum's Universal Robots)—a science fiction play written by Čapek in 1920 and premiered in 1921. The work became a historic milestone by introducing the word "robot" into the global vocabulary, forever changing science fiction and technology. The term was suggested to the author by his brother, Josef Čapek, based on the Czech word *robota*, meaning forced labor or servitude.
In 1942: Isaac Asimov—an American writer of Russian origin—was widely considered one of the greatest science fiction authors of all time and the spiritual father of modern robotics. He formally introduced the term "robotics" and formulated the Three Laws of Robotics in his science fiction stories.

Robótica industrial:

1954 to 1958s: The first digital, programmable robotic arm in history was the Unimate, created by American inventor George Devol in 1954. Devol patented the device under the name "Programmed Article Transfer." Shortly thereafter, in 1956, he partnered with engineer and entrepreneur Joseph Engelberger to found Unimation, the world's first robotics company.
1961: The Unimate, the first industrial robot patented by Devol and built with Joseph Engelberger, was installed on a General Motors assembly line.
1970s to 1990s: The advent of microprocessors and computational intelligence brought autonomy and expanded the use of robots in medicine, space exploration, and industry.
